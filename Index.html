Remy's ConsultingTechSoup Integration — Architecture Proposal
Integration architecture proposal

From manual handoff to a connected intake

Remy's Consulting has worked with TechSoup for about three years, and TechSoup remains an important source of visibility with nonprofit organizations. Today, moving a nonprofit's information from that first submission into Remy's existing client workflow still depends on someone reviewing it and, often, typing it into HoneyBook by hand. This document lays out where that manual work happens, what should stay exactly as it is, and the simplest way to connect the pieces so information travels on its own.

What's already working

Remy's already has a HoneyBook automation that runs well once a record exists: it creates the client/project record, tags it as coming from TechSoup, sends the right emails and materials, and carries the person through follow-up. None of that needs to change. The only gap is what happens before that record exists.

What we are — and aren't — building

Not building

  • A new CRM
  • A custom database
  • A custom frontend
  • A new automation platform
  • Complex middleware
  • A rebuilt HoneyBook automation
  • A duplicate intake form, unless required

Building

  • A clean intake handoff
  • A HubSpot connection
  • Zapier automation
  • Required data mapping
  • Duplicate-aware logic
  • The HoneyBook handoff
  • Testing and documentation

Cost and maintenance philosophy

The architecture is designed around the tools Remy's already uses, to minimize new subscriptions, custom infrastructure, and ongoing maintenance. The final recurring cost depends on the existing HubSpot, Zapier, and HoneyBook plans and the exact steps required — that will be verified before production deployment, rather than promised in advance.

If a step fails, Zapier records the failed task and the account's configured notifications alert the team, who can investigate and retry through Zapier's normal controls. For this phase, native platform monitoring is preferred over building a separate error-management system.

Security & data governance

This integration moves nonprofit and contact information between organizations. Before anything goes live, both sides should confirm: authorization to connect TechSoup/HubSpot to Zapier, exactly what information may be transferred, which HoneyBook data is necessary, account access and permissions, and any retention or privacy requirements. To confirm — this document makes no legal claims; data-sharing permissions must be confirmed by the organizations before production use.

Reference

Plain-language glossary

CRM
Short for "customer relationship management" — the system that stores contact and organization records, in this case HubSpot.
Zap
A single automated recipe in Zapier: when this happens, do that.
Trigger
The event that starts a Zap or a HoneyBook automation — for example, a new contact being created.
Action
What happens once a trigger fires — for example, creating a record in HoneyBook.
API
A defined way for two systems to exchange information automatically, without a person copying it by hand.
Webhook
A message one system sends automatically to another the moment something happens.
Automation
A sequence of steps a system runs on its own once triggered — no manual action required.
Duplicate
The same person or organization accidentally recorded more than once.
Field mapping
Deciding which piece of information in one system corresponds to which field in another.
